Vote by mail

Apply for a mail ballot at City Hall, 11995 Haney Place. Ballots became available October 7. Your completed ballot must be received by the Chief Election Officer by 8 pm on October 17. Follow the return instructions in your package.

The deadline is for receipt. Posting your ballot by October 17 is not enough. The City advises against relying on regular mail if it could arrive late. Call the election office at 604-467-7423 to confirm an approved drop-off option.

Eligibility and ID

Resident voters must be Canadian citizens, at least 18 on October 17, resident in BC for six months before registering, living in Maple Ridge, and legally entitled to vote. Eligible renters can vote too.

You can register or update your voter information when voting. For either, bring two accepted ID documents, including one with your signature. If neither proves your address, a residency declaration is required.

If you are already registered and your details are current, you make a declaration to receive a ballot; the City says documentation is not required.

Some non-resident property owners also qualify under additional rules. Contact the election office about eligibility and accepted identification.

Accessibility and help

Ask for help marking your ballot, bring a translator, or request curbside voting if a physical disability or impaired mobility prevents entry. A person assisting or translating must complete a declaration.

Three recreation borrowing questions

Voters will decide three separate Yes/No questions about borrowing for recreation facilities. Each proposal allows a borrowing term of up to 25 years.

Proposed borrowing limits · summary of the three ballot questions
ProjectMaximum borrowingLoan bylaw

Albion Twin-Rink Arena Expansion Maximum borrowing$143 million Loan bylaw8122-2026

Each question passes if more than half its valid votes are Yes. You can make a different choice for each project.

A Yes result gives borrowing approval. The incoming Council must still consider adopting the relevant bylaw. If adopted, detailed design and further engagement follow. If a question fails, that borrowing does not proceed.

Who has the best chance of winning?

In the sources reviewed through October 12, we did not find a public Kok–Ruimy voting-intention poll with published fieldwork dates, sample size and methodology.

No reliable numerical odds yet. The evidence reviewed does not establish a statistically supported favourite. This page does not assign winning percentages.

Dan Ruimy's position

Ruimy has been mayor since 2022 and previously served as a Member of Parliament. He is seeking another term with A Better Maple Ridge.

He has the record and visibility of an incumbent, plus a demonstrated citywide victory in 2022. That does not show how many of those voters will support him again.

Jerry Kok's position

Kok is a former president of the Ridge Meadows Chamber of Commerce. His candidate profile describes a business and municipal advisory-committee background.

In this two-candidate race, he is the alternative to the incumbent. Without current polling, the size of his support is unmeasured here.

What could change the assessment?

A transparent local poll with fieldwork dates, sample size and methodology would be more useful than online popularity surveys or campaign claims. Turnout and voters' assessment of the candidates' records and proposals will matter; previous totals cannot simply be transferred to 2026.

There is also insufficient candidate-level polling here to estimate individual council or school-trustee winning chances. These are separate contests for multiple seats.
